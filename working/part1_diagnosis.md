# Phase 2 Output — Part 1 §1 Diagnosis (drafted)

## Primary breakdown

**The first-purchase voucher for a local service is never converted into a planned action.** A buyer completes checkout with vague future intent ("I should get a massage sometime"), receives a receipt-framed confirmation, and is dropped back into deal-browsing. Without a commitment moment in the first 48 hours — a chosen date, a booking, a calendar hold — salience decays. By the time the user surfaces redemption intent, the context is cold, the next step feels uncertain, and the deal has to re-win attention against everything else in their life. That's the loop that produces the >10-day median TTR, the "How do I use this?" support contacts arriving *late* in the window, and the refund-on-expiry tail.

**This is the root, not a symptom**, because it's the only explanation that fits all four signals simultaneously: (a) declining redemption in local but not goods (goods has no "decide when" step — the package arrives), (b) a rising median TTR (intent is decaying, not just timing-out), (c) a top support driver that's about *use*, not *purchase* (users surface confusion when they finally try to act), and (d) refunds correlated with unredeemed-plus-unclear (deals expire unused, friction surfaces, the user asks for their money back). A pure-clarity diagnosis would predict support contacts to spike *early*; a pure-friction diagnosis would predict a sharp redemption cliff near expiry. We see neither — we see the signature of decay.

## Secondary

**Per-deal-type next-step ambiguity** — a generic "redeem" surface for what are really four different actions (book online, phone the merchant, pick a date on a partner site, walk in). This is real, and it's evident in user reviews where buyers default to phoning the business directly. It's secondary because it's a *necessary enabler* of the primary fix, not load-bearing alone. A beautifully written per-deal-type help page, shipped without a commitment mechanic, moves support contacts but not repeat-purchase rate. A commitment mechanic, shipped without per-deal-type specificity, can't function (you can't add a calendar hold without picking a time). The two ship together; the commitment is the lever, the deal-type specificity is what makes the lever pullable.

## Explicitly out of scope

Merchant reliability (overbooking, no-shows, refusing to honor) is real, surfaces clearly in public reviews, and contributes to refunds. It is **not in this strategy's first 90 days**. The Post-Purchase team doesn't own merchant quality, and trying to fix it from a post-purchase seat dilutes the focus. Naming it explicitly so it doesn't quietly creep into scope.
